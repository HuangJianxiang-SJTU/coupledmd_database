# Final revision figure captions

Fig. 1 Frozen cohort, release boundary and cohort composition. (A) Representative snapshot of the GPCR–G protein complex embedded in a homogeneous POPC bilayer from the MD simulation. The protein is colored by secondary structure, POPC lipids are shown as green sticks, and the simulation box is outlined in green. Water and ions are omitted for clarity. (B) Distribution of the 207 included systems across GPCR classes and G-protein families. (C) Disposition of the 222-record working inventory into 207 included, 13 unresolved and two excluded records. (D) Counts of released systems, computational repeats, receptor-name entities and mapped UniProt accessions. Family composition is descriptive and should not be interpreted as physiological coupling prevalence.

Fig. 2 Molecular-record boundary, data model and access layers. (A) Reduction of full-system simulations containing the protein complex, membrane and ions (archived without water coordinates) into matched PDB/XTC records retaining the selected protein-complex representation. (B) Supported uses (Y), supplied summaries (S) and unsupported uses (—) for reduced trajectories, gateway summaries and full-system source coordinates; solvent-dependent analyses are unsupported at every level because the archived full-system trajectories contain no water coordinates. (C) Hierarchical relationship among system, replica and checksum-bearing file records. (D) Access through Supplementary Data, the CoupledMD web portal and REST API (www.coupledmd.cn), the CoupledMD GitHub repositories and the Zenodo deposition.

Fig. 3 Replica-level structural stability. (A–C) Distributions across computational repeats of receptor transmembrane-core Cα RMSD P95, Gα-interface Cα RMSD P95, and initial-interface-contact retention P05, respectively. Structural metrics are available for 618 of the 621 expected replica records. The complete observed ranges, including high Gα-interface RMSD and low contact-retention values, are displayed without clipping or outlier annotations. These are full-window metrics; Supplementary Data S7 provides TM-core stationarity flags and discard recommendations, which do not establish stationarity of the interface metrics.

Fig. 4 Recovery of structurally defined peptide-binding sites. (A) Ranked best peptide-site pocket frequency for 58 peptide-binding-site controls, colored by G-protein family. Nine controls without a recovered peptide-proximal persistent cluster are shown at zero. The dashed line denotes the 0.85 grid-frequency criterion used to define persistent pocket volume; it is distinct from the cohort-level recovery fraction of 49 of 58 controls (84.5%). The unoccupied 0.1–0.8 frequency interval is omitted using an explicit axis break. (B) Family-resolved ranked distributions for Gi/o (n=18), Gs (n=18), and Gq/11 (n=22), using the same broken frequency axis and threshold; vertical position denotes within-family rank. No G12/13 system met the peptide-control definition. This analysis tests geometric recovery of peptide-contact regions and should not be interpreted as a benchmark of small-molecule-pocket recovery.

Fig. 5 Worked example for contact persistence and checksum-first reuse. (A) Contact-frequency heatmap and persistence bars for the 30 most frequently contacting of 48 S1P-contacting residues in G12_7T6B; results for all 48 residues are retained in the source data. (B) Checksum-first reuse workflow showing the system-specific replica-1 PDB and XTC SHA-256 digests, the complete 48-residue MDAnalysis selection and validation of the expected atom and frame counts. (C) Binding-pocket Cα RMSD trajectory for the worked-example system. The pocket trajectory includes the initial rearrangement: the operational pocket RMSD stationarity onset is 150 ns and the final-100-ns mean is 1.54 Å. The full-window statistics and contacts in this example include the initial segment; they are not post-discard equilibrium estimates.

Fig. 6 Structural-validation metrics stratified by G-protein family and GPCR class. Violin and jittered point distributions of receptor TM-core Cα RMSD P95 (left), Gα-interface Cα RMSD P95 (middle), and initial-interface-contact retention P05 (right), stratified by G-protein family (top: Gs, n = 192; Gi/o, n = 285; Gq/11, n = 123; G12/13, n = 18 replica records) and GPCR class (bottom: A, n = 540; B, n = 78). Horizontal bars show medians. The 618 records represent computational repeats nested within 206 systems; they are not 618 independent systems. Metrics summarize the original full production windows. The plots are descriptive and do not establish equivalence or family- or class-specific biological differences. Supplementary Data S7 provides TM-core stationarity flags and discard recommendations; these do not establish stationarity of the interface metrics shown here.

Figure S1: Receptor representation across G-protein families. Circular dendrogram of 174 receptor names clustered by Jaccard distance and average linkage using binary family-presence profiles. Leaves are labeled by representative PDB identifiers and colored by G-protein family; gray denotes receptors represented with multiple families. The asterisk identifies Gs_8HTI, which lacks a mapped UniProt accession. Branch proximity reflects release coverage only, not sequence or structural similarity.

Figure S2: CoupledMD web portal. (A) Landing page with cohort summary organized by G-protein family. (B) Representative system (G12_7T6B) page with embedded browser-based viewer and precomputed annotation layers. (C) Interactive REST API documentation.

Figure S3: Reduced-release quality control and file scale. (A) Full-frame quality-control results for nine structural and file-integrity checks across 207 systems in each of three release replicas. The panel’s legacy label “Chain break absent” denotes absence of periodic chain-split artifacts—consecutive-residue Cα pairs separated by more than 8.0 Å only before minimum-image correction—and is not a physical chain-rupture test. A PASS cell indicates that all 207 records within that replica satisfy the corresponding criterion; collectively, all 621 replica records (207 systems × 3 replicas) pass all nine criteria. (B) Distribution of reduced XTC file sizes across the three replicas. (C) Distribution of retained protein-complex atom counts across the three replicas. Each violin represents 207 system-level records.

Figure S4: Operational TM-core RMSD stationarity across the release. (A) Distribution of teq for 589 passing replicas, stratified by G-protein family; the 29 nonstationary replicas are shown separately beyond an axis break and have no assigned teq. (B) Cumulative distributions using either the 589 passing replicas or all 618 evaluated replicas as denominator; the all-evaluated curve reaches 95.3%. (C) Distribution of the maximum passing-replica teq per system for 206 systems (median, 250 ns; 90th percentile, 430 ns). This recommendation excludes nonstationary replicas: 26 systems have at least one flagged replica. Supplementary Data S7 supplies those flags and counts. Three Gs_8HTI records could not be assessed because their TM-core selection is unavailable. The criterion detects residual RMSD drift and does not establish convergence of all observables. Applying the windowed test to Gα-interface RMSD, with a 1.0 Å plateau tolerance (0.5 Å for the TM core) and the same slope limit of 0.1 Å per 100 ns, flagged 289 of 618 evaluated replicas (46.8%) as nonstationary. Supplementary Data S7 provides discard recommendations for the receptor TM core only; no cohort-wide interface discard is recommended. These recommendations do not establish interface stationarity; interface analyses require their own assessment and may require excluding trajectories or extending sampling. The full-window interface RMSD summaries are descriptive, not estimates from a demonstrated stationary ensemble.
